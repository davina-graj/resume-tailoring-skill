# Action Verbs for Computer Science Resumes

Source: Georgia Tech Career Center's recommended action verbs for CS/technical
resumes. Used by `matching-strategies.md` Strategy 5 (Action Verb Alignment)
when writing or reframing bullets in Phase 3/4.1.

Every bullet should open with a verb from the category matching what the
bullet is actually about — not reused mechanically bullet-to-bullet. Rotate
within a category across a resume's bullets so the same verb doesn't repeat.

## Technical Development & Programming

Use for: writing, shipping, or improving code/systems.

Developed, Engineered, Implemented, Built, Programmed, Coded, Automated,
Optimized, Refactored, Debugged

## Design & Architecture

Use for: system/data/UX design decisions, planning before building.

Designed, Architected, Devised, Created, Crafted, Formulated

## Collaboration & Communication

Use for: cross-functional work, teamwork, presenting, writing docs.

Collaborated, Partnered, Communicated, Presented, Documented, Explained

## Leadership & Initiative

Use for: owning a project/team, starting something, mentoring.

Led, Managed, Initiated, Directed, Organized, Mentored, Facilitated

## Impact & Results

Use for: the outcome/metric a bullet proves, usually the second verb or the
clause after the comma (e.g. "Refactored the ingestion pipeline, **reducing**
latency by 40%").

Achieved, Improved, Increased, Decreased, Streamlined, Enhanced, Solved

## Matching a bullet to a category

1. Identify what the bullet is fundamentally about (not just its first word
   in the source material) — building something, designing it, working with
   others on it, leading it, or the result it produced.
2. Pick the verb from that category that most precisely matches the real
   action (e.g. "Refactored" vs "Developed" — don't default to the most
   generic verb in the category if a more specific one fits).
3. Most strong bullets combine two categories: an opening verb (Technical/
   Design/Collaboration/Leadership) + an Impact & Results verb for the
   outcome clause.
4. Never swap in a verb that overstates what happened (truth-preserving
   principle still applies) — "Led" requires actual ownership, not just
   participation; use "Collaborated"/"Partnered" when the role was
   contributing, not directing.
